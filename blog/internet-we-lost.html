The Internet we lost, the Software that we Loved and the Future that we Fear

Many younger people may not know, some may not remember and other may have forgotten on purpose to not being drag by sadness. The internet in the 90’s was wild, uncensored, horribly designed, difficult to navigate…

People who were born in the 2000 may be asking: so it was awful, right? No, my boy. It was exciting! It was wild, crazy sometimes, with more than questionable design decisions (blinking stuff and saturated bright colors), and not always useful, as it was scattered and not indexed and you could try to find about something in Yahoo, Lycos or later Terra (in Spain, at least) and it was a gamble. You could end up finding some boring stuff or something totally unrelated… or you could find gold! Unrelated to what you wanted, but gold nonetheless. It had this feeling of discovery and excitement that it is long gone.

And there were chats. First was IRC. That was a truly new experience for those of us who grew up with phones attached to the walls and computers that barely had a few colors for games, mostly used for accountability and word processing in companies. Suddenly, from your room you could be chatting with hundreds of people that were in many places, real time, different experiences, different hobbies… I remember one night chatting with a few guys about some airplanes from WWII. Probably none of us had actually much knowledge, but it was amazing!

However, I think the most important thing I feel is missing was that intuition that we were witnessing somethign that would shock the world, something that would take us to that uncanny future we read in comics and scifi books. It was a time where every other year a new amazing processor was released, a graphic card, new game technology that showed graphics that we didn’t think were possible… I cannot remember how many times I said to some friend “ok, graphics cannot get better than this!” and every time I was wrong.

Is this just nostalgia? I am sure there is some, mixed with the fact that the development of technology was at its begining, so there was a lot of room for improvement.

However, I used to believe that we could have had a useful internet like today on top of that feeling of discovery and excitement, of freedom. The problem is that business and governments didn’t really like tha freedom for everyone. The first ones wanted to get the money and the second ones… well, they prefer centralized communication media.

So I wonder, would it be possible to have a balance in between? I mean, there are still people out there creating things (I refuse to use the term content, fucking SEOs, marketing drones and corporate tools corrupted it) just for the shake of it, for the fun, for the enjoyment. Like this blog that almost no one reads, and almost I don’t update.

The Software we love

If we talk about those days, we have to talk about software. Do you know that in the past, you could buy software that came in a box, with manuals and without breaking bugs? It was a different time, if you shipped a program and it had bugs that prevented it to work correctly, you would go under. There was no way to deliver updates or hotfixes in a way that every customer could get it. And probably the users would not be happy about it anyway. I get that.

But you owned your software. Did you buy this version of the text processor, this game or this utility? It is yours, no more payments. Do you want to upgrade? Well, add some small money and get it, no full price again.

What do we have now? Software as a service or as a subscription. You do not buy software, but the license for its use. In the cases where you buy the software, you get updates and hotfixes since day 1 because business idiots made decisions that it had to release that day. If you get boxes there won’t be manuals, but usually no boxes at all.

And slop enters the game, many programmers do not know enough to make really good solid software (I would not put me out of that group) and worst of all, they do not really have incentives nor time to do so. “Ship fast and break things!” is a great plan when you are coming up with a new revolutionary idea… and you have the talent and knowledge to do so with quality. But now, that mentality has permeated to everywhere thanks to people who don’t even know what a compiler is. Just because they would get a bonus or a promotion if they increase sales or reduce costs in the short term. The long term, the future we want to dream about? Fuck that, they are here to squeeze every euro they can until they get fired or move up the chain.

Ok, ok, let’s calm down a bit. This gets me carried away…

Internet and software

Initially, distributing software using internet is a net positive. More people can be reached, you can provide better support or bug fixes faster. Because how could you fix a bug in a software that is burnt in a CD (or any of the ancient physical media)?

It was a revolution, really. Not only you could get cheaper software, just from home without having to go to the shop or waiting for the delivery. It makes sense, software is just a sequence of bits ordered in a particular way, so sending them through the net, that was designed for precisely that, was the most logical thing to do.

And I am pretty sure that the first time a user got an update that fixed a problem with the software, it felt like a moment where you could taste the future.

Then it came another idea: providing not the software as a final product, but as a service to access. That was great! And bear with me through the memory lane:

In the 90’s, buying a CD of music was kind of expensive. 20 euros for 9 or 10 songs. And the worst part was that you only listened to the single, the one that you liked, on MTV or other tv shows. You are young, you don’t have much money and you love to listen to music. Pirating it using eMule is a thing that only criminals and evil people do, you are told. Besides, you like that group, you want to support them, so you take the leap and buy the CD. And you only like the song you already know. It was unfair.

So eventually, everyone was downloading something and companies were furious.

“Hey, what about providing cheaper options? a better deal for your customers instead of telling them they suck and are criminals?”.

“No, no, no”, said John McCorpo. “It is expensive, but fair, a lot investment to be done…”

Well, how about you replicate something like Napster or other pirate sites do? That would reduce costs and…”

“You criminal! Shut up and pay!”

And then, Apple Music. That slapped the face of McBusiness truly hard. It opened the way to a future that made sense to everyone who already used internet and computers. Spotify came later. Movie companies were still thinking “That is not our problem, you cannot really do that with movies…”. “Hi, I am Netflix. Fuck off”.

On top of that, saving money in creating the physical media, burn it, packaging, delivering… would help you reduce costs, being scalable and, eventually, reducing the price of software, right? … right??! … RIGHT??!

Well, John McMoney and other business people figured out that the part of “saving money in creating and delivering software” was great, but the part of “reducing the price tag” was not so interesting. And they created a school of though that evolved up to our days which says that the future should be purely digital to save costs and problems, and by that, increasing prices bit by bit would make customers unaware of the scam. That school of thought have evolved even more and other geniuses of scam said “digital games should be licenses only and you should pay 80$ or more”. And day-1 patches of the same size of the original game, which means, they rewrote almost everything because it was truly broken.

In all fairness, it was not just a pull to squeeze all the money they could, but the idea that a company had to ship fast and early to avoid competitors to gain market. And what that entails is:

  • Deadlines decided by non technical people. Spreadsheet people, with their fancy presentations about churn, ROI and quarterly proyections that have no idea about the technical problems or difficulties to create quality software.
  • Due to the previous point, software teams being forced to slap frameworks and dependencies to avoid investing time in creating supporting libraries and modules needed for the product, adding a lot of unnecessary and unknown code into their products. The infamous software bloat.
  • Lost of knowledge about how systems work. That makes a bigger problem as it is more difficult to find people who knows, and make it harder for developers to learn what others before them already knew.

And finally, they discovered the best strategy is subscription instead of buying software. You can predict the income, the investments, and so on, because your users are captive of your software.

And it is fine, don’t get me wrong. It has plenty of benefits to the user, in principle. However, once you don’t own the software, it is more difficult to opt out. Unless there is another competitor that offers what you really need, you usually have to deal with the bullshit they introduce. Or worst yet, with the functionality they take out.

The great dissater is that people in charge of software companies ended up being spreadsheet people. They don’t have a clue about technical knowledge, they believe they know better than tech and users, and beyond all, they are always trying to push bullshit into users, things the users don’t need or taking out things they want behind a premium paywall. Because their goal is to increase the numbers in their spreadsheets and make their graphics grow green in their presentations. Let quality be damned!

The Future that we Fear

If you grew up in the 80’s or the 90’s, The Future was hopeful, full of neon and optimistic. We could not wait for it to arrive! Microcomputers gave us the first taste of such future and let our imagination fly with its 8-bit games. Internet changed our lives forever and, at the time at least, for the better. Mobile phones… Tecnology was amazing, it was getting better each time, simple to use, fun…

We imagined hyperconnectivity with friends and a wrist band with an holographic screen to talk with your friends… People building technology actually loved technology. They were there for the money, but also for the fun and excitement. They did things because they wanted those things to exist. They wanted to push the technology to its limits and get closer to that future we dreamt of.

Younger generations could not really grasp what it meant watching the presentation for the iPod. We were using portable CD players that, in the best cases, could read mp3 so you could have more than 20 songs. And I think maybe some small players that were memory sticks with buttons. You had to know what you had to navigate effectively. And iPod shocked the world. The technology was capable of that.

Who decides what to build now

Normies, MBAs, bureochrats and uninspired people that see technology as a means to make money. There is no vision, no passion. Only quaterly reports, bonuses, numbers in spreadsheets and powerpoints. Those are the ones making decisions on what to build and what is not useful or needed. Improve code base and efficiency? No, it will hurt the bonus, better to add more features ignoring the ineficciencies. People will buy new computer, new phone, new something. So whatever.

What about doing something cool that nobody really gave a shit about, but would be cool to do? Nah, again that cannot be used for promotion or to make the bonus bigger. And the worst case is that these people want to impress the people above them who are the same. So showing some amazing development, even if useful for tech fans and common people, if it cannot be converted into more money per quarter, it will not impress them. Technology industry is not so much about technology these days, but about how that technology can make more money to the company clients that are companies themselves.

Don’t you believe me? Well, think about Netflix and other streaming services. How did we end up with services that are so shitty and expensive, full of movies/series that nobody wanted being pushed down our throats? How did they ended up being frustrating because they have nothing you wanted in the first place? Why do they add advertisement in the pay subscription? Are we not paying already precisely because we didn’t want the adds? And why, after a decade or more where piracy of movies and series was marginal, irrelevant, it has piked again and it is at all times high (this may not be true, read it as hyperbole)?

And what about Windows? I mean, it is not like Microsoft was a loved company per se, but for sure was respected and the software they produced was well built or worked more or less fine. Now, with Windows 11, they want to put adds in the system as well as everything AI. And the system itself is plagued with a kind of bugs and errors that makes no sense for the company. Windows always had some problems here and there, but if we are honest, it always was a reliable operative system. It does not feel like that anymore, with the new window manager having freezes, bugs, weird behaviors… critical bugs in the updates, in the Task Manager…

And I don’t want to talk about Xbox department and how it destroyed itself and the game market for that matter.

The above cases are just the result of accumulative decissions made with short-term vision, the same people who make those decissions do not look in the long term, because nowadays there is no long term in tech when it comes to be in a company for years. They look for short term bonuses, medium term options to jump to a higher position. That’s it. And technology, you say? Yeah, that’s my point.

A New Hope

We used to be optimistic and looked at technology as something good and amazing. And the good thing is that the technology (the hardware, the devices) is already here. So it is a matter of fixing how software works and also how software companies operate. Not an easy feat, I know. But there are glimpses of hope.

First of all, SpaceX, Tesla and HyperLink. Yes, Elon is a controversial figure and he is not the one that writes code or build things. But the mentality towards technology and software is the correct one: use it to improve our lives, provide better services and products and, if there is some luck, improve humanity lives as a whole. The same way Linux did, or Internet.

And I think his influence in the change of mentality will be important for software and technology. Without regard of his ideas finally triumph or not, whether you hate him or not.

Also DHH, the creator of Ruby on Rails and founder of Basecamp with its Omarchy Linux setup. I didn’t try it, maybe in the future. But it reignited the passion for computers for a lot of people, some of them not neccessary developers, but tech enthusiasts that had lost the passion over the last iterations of computers, Windows and phones. And it may be just a small thing related to a tech bubble where I live in, but it may be something more.

And as a last example of hope, Steam and its SteamOS. I claim that most people who use Windows in their homes do so because most games were Windows only. Most people do not need Office, as Open Office (or Libre Office) could cover their needs. Most people uses browsers and games on their computers. And SteamOS, originally developed for steam decks and for only playing, may end up being an OS with all its functionalities. And that can change a lot. I still believe that Steam is full of very talented and motivated people who love software and technology.

What do we do, in the meantime?

In my case, I have been a developer for over 10 years. It is not much, considering I started late. However, I am still trying to be better, to know more, to improve. And I do that because I love technology, I am an enthusiat, and I would love to be able to do good software, even if for a company of for my own small projects.

And that is what other developers should also strive towards, if they really care. Do not despair!

And for users? Ask more, do not settle for bad software. Do not settle for bad services. Complain, search for alternatives, cancel services that does not serve you anymore. Reject the crap!

We may still be able to win…
