Just Another Cycle of Hype

Or what poeople like to call it, another bubble. I am talking about AI. And my take, tl;dr is: this is another DotCom bubble, but with new technology.

If we take a look at the Dot Com bubble there are some points we can take as an example to try to understand what will be the future like. First of it, after the bubble we will have AI that will be really useful and sustainable. The rest of AI companies, products and companies will dissapear.

Since the begining

I cannot claim "I said so" because I don't think I wrote about it anywhere. But the reality is, and always was, that these new class of AIs are based on LLM. And LLM just look for correlations between words, paragraphs, sections... to predict what comes next. And they need data to be able to output something. If it is not in the data, it is unlikely that it could create anything really new. And after the inicial shock and awe, and I won't lie, the first feeling of fear about what the future could bring to us developers, I came to that conclussion and I said so in many conversations with other colleagues and firends more invested in the AI trend.

The reality is that many jobs will be affected, and I am pretty sure that there will be some kind of revolution associated to LLMs. I have no clue about how it will look. However, I think we can claim now that it will not eliminate every job. And for sure, it will not eliminate software developers. Although there are nuances that I would like to talk about.

AGI and the Quest for General Inteligence

One question that I was going around since 2 years ago or so was about LLMs could achieve AGI or not. Based on what LLMs are, my first though was "No, it is not possible". However... what is intelligence?

For me, that was the main question: we barely know what intelligence is or how to properly defined it. So I started to think that, perhaps, what we called intelligence, that thing that we humans are so proud of, could be just doing the same thing LLMs are doing: correlate million of concepts, finding patterns, creating a result based on that. If that were the case, LLMs actually had a chance to achieve intelligence, and hence, consciusness. We don't know what is intelligence, just its apparent effects. And until recently, it was very apparent that AI models were not able to simulate it. LLMs changed that and condused everyone. Many people even though it was AGI. But that says more about our own ignorance that about the "intelligence" of LLMs.

The Revolution and Destruction of Jobs

Yes, there will be jobs that will dissapear and some already started to dissapear. Also in software development. But hear me out.

There are many jobs that, if they are not yet impacted, soon will be. I am not sure if the impact will be so hard as to make those jobs dissappear, but for sure reduce the amount of people needed for them. Copywriting, translating and anything that requires text could reduce most of the workers with just one and an LLM. Those a re the easy ones, but there are likely plenty of positions along the corporations and goverments of the world that are what could be considered "bullshit jobs". I wrote about them in the past (not in here, somewhere else) and I was not even the first one or the smartest one to do so.

But those are not the only ones to be affected. We have been reading everywhere that software development was going to de extint pretty soon. And companies were listening, which explains in part the job market in those fields in the last two years. It has been hard, also due to the huge amount of people who where laid off after COVID and during the AI peak. Companies where cautious, thinking a lot when and where to hire, just in case the LLMs actually delivered and they would not need developers.

Well, I think today we can more or less be safe saying that it will not be like that. Probably there will be some surge in developers to fix some craziness that was introduced by the prompt engineers and vibe coders. It seems developers are not going to go away yet. However, things have changed and it is important to recognize that.

I recognize that there may be positions that are heavily impacted. However, if a programming position has been impacted to the point of dissapearing, I would guess it was not really programming. What do I mean by that? What I mean is that creating a web page with some frameworks may be just connecting and defining the elements themshelves using programming. But I would have some problems to call that programming. It would be more like following Ikea instructions to mount furniture. Those positions are going to dissapear as individual positions. It will come as a part of another job, something you do as a part of your daily job. And probably just using an LLM and tweking some details will be enough.

But if you write severs, solve technical problems with code, plugins, etc. I would say you will have a job.

And the Bubble

So now it seems AI companies, that were raising money like it was nothing, seem to be starting to have problems keeping the trend. Not only that, but the monetization is starting to be a problem. The numbers are brutal: the most known ones didn't make it even yet and the prices, that were artificially low in order to get market share, are not going to be sustainable. It sounds like the Dot Com bubble, isn't it? So something have to happen.

Some people say that the real burst of the bubble will come when one of the big ones delcare bankrupcy. Or is adquired by a competitor. Another problem I heard they have is that, contrary to the mayority of software business models out there, LLMs are not scalable. More users means more servers, more power and more computation per token and per request. It seems that this will not last much, as it also seems that the VC money that was fueling this race is drying out.

As when the web exploded, no one knew how the future of the web was going to be or what model it would have to make money (anyone imagined... just advertisement?? I didn't). For sure this will not go away and somehow things will be different than they where 5 years ago. All I hope is that it will be better, hopefully some mindsets change and we change the way we create sofware, we start to really take advantage of the hardware we have and the programs work better and more efficiently. But I don't know, I just can dream...
