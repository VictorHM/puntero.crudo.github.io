Our Memory is not The Reality

Did you notice, at some point in your life, that a memory of some event ended up changing, even in your own recollection? I realized that years ago and it was surprising. I am kind of a story-teller, and one of the main abilities for being one is to create a narrative about the story, diminish parts of the story and amplify others in order to make the story appealing and interesting.

However, as you keep telling the same story, you keep improving it, stressing more and more the important parts and skipping the irrelevant parts. But notice this: the irrelevant parts are not irrelevant in what happened, they are "irrelevant" in regards of the story telling. And that's what I noticed: stories evolve and change as you keep learning what works and what not, they are still the same basic facts, but the way they are told make them more important than they were in reality.

And then, I discovered that our memories are totally unreliable. I mean, psychology already knew that so I didn't discover any fundamental truth. However that was still a surprise to me.

Memories define who we think we are. Maybe not who we are, but they are important to our own image and self indentity. And many people may still think what I did before discovering this: that memories are more or less reliable and what we remember is closed to what really happened. It is not true. Memories are narratives and they are defined by our own narrative of who we think we are. The ones that are embarrasing for us, tend to be modified in order to minimize the bad image about us. The ones that are good for our self image, get improved. And so the Story of Our Life is fundamentally flawed.

Perfect Memory

Why is this? Does it means that our memory system is broken and badly designed? Well, no, I don't think so. Actually I believe that is the perfect way to make us survive. You may be wondering if I lost my mind, but please check the stories of people with a perfect memory. It is considered a defect, or a problem. And the reason is that remembering things in perfect detail makes those people suffer greatly and not completely functional.

I am sure some of you are thinking that it sounds like an amazing thing. Being able to remember what happened, when, how etc with perfect detail. But it is a curse. You will remember the sad moments of your life as if they happened a minute ago. Think about it for a second: a love one pass away and you will remember it as if it happened a minute ago... for every second of your life. That love that broke your heart? Yeah, it happened a minute ago. That mistake that changed your life? No way of moving on because regret will feel fresh as if you just made it. People with that affection would give everything just for being able to forget.

So the mechanism of narratives, as imperfect as it may be, is a way to protect ourselves and survive. It is a survival mechanism. Maybe it was just the way it evolved, not a solution to a problem. But the fact that there are people that can remember everything with perfect detail makes me think that it may actually be an evolutionary adaptation, or just the winning mutation compared to the mutation for perfect memory. However, the key part I am amazed, and may like to investigate for my curiosity, is the fact that the brain seems to be capable of remembering everything with perfect detail. Or at least, the potentiallity for it. Amazing!
