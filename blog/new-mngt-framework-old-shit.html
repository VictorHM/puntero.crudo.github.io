New Development Frameworks, Same Old Shit

A new framework has been imposed in another company. And you receive a link to the document that describes how, when and where it will change things… apparently. But when you read it, it is a generic document that could be applied to ANY company. Nothing specific of the company you work from, nothing particular or adapted to the way the company works. A document that the people working on it took a few months to finish, but that you could think it was a template from the internet to apply to your company. But someone forgot to adapt it.

Fucking generic managers that think they don’t need to know anything about software development or what works and what not in the teams. “Naaah, let’s cramp all of you together like you are simply numbers and resources!”. Later, there will be post-mortem documents on why the last release failed, why it underperformed with customers and why the more experienced developers moved on to find greener pastures… and it will never point to the real cause: the imposition of a generic framework, to call it some way, into a company without any thoughts put into it. The managers that did it will never face the direct consequences of their incompetence, nor will even realize they are such a thing at all. It will be something like the market, the developers and what not.

What New Framework is This?

It doesn’t matter. It is a new one “invented” (meaning copy-pasting) by another grifter to sell it to executives with empty promises about how it will help their company. No matter that it is the same shit again, with some ideas from Agile manifesto to look modern and different. But once you pass over the overwhelming disgust and tedium of reading it, it is clear as daylight that it is a new flavor of Scrum.

And there will be some asking: “wait, but Scrum is Agile, right?”

No. It is the corruption of Agile ideas to allow the creator (one of the original creators of Agile, if I remember correctly) to make money and to allow managers to go back to waterfall style of management without having to say it openly. If you though differently, welcome to the other side.

The problem with generic good-for-everything structures

Why do we end up with such things like these frameworks and documents? I think it is due to the obsession to create something that can apply to any situation. When you are trying to think in a very abstract way, to try to capture every possible situation, case or variation to generate some product that could apply to any situation… well, you have to be extremely generic, or painfully detailed, and you may still loose some situation that you didn’t expect.

These people didn’t write code in decades, if ever. So, what problem are they trying to solve? A management problem, not development one.

And with “workflow frameworks” if we call them so, that is one of the main problems. The other one is that the people designing these things are not writing code, designing specific systems or solving practical problems. These people didn’t write code in decades, if ever. So, what problem are they trying to solve? A management problem, not development one. And, in my opinion, that explains why no matter how much they claim something is Agile, development-focused or whatever other marketing stunt, every single time it ends up being a convenient tool for upper management to constrain everything into the same waterfall style we have been suffering since ever. It is not like in the 60s or 70s, but it is still the same approach.

Why? Solutions?

Why? Well, because if we like it or not, businesses need to have some predictive control over the products or tools they produce. And the moment a company grows big enough, decision-making people cannot see directly what each team is doing due to size and impracticality, is the moment it “needs” to bend more towards the waterfall style, with deadlines, top-to-bottom decisions, etc.

Solution? Honestly, no idea. I see two conflicts of interest that are very difficult to align. There are some approaches that worked for some companies,Spotify comes to my mind now as well as BaseCamp, but I am not sure it can be valid for every other company. So yes, this was mainly a rant, I hope you enjoyed it ;)
