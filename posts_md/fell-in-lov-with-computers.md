# When I fell in Love with Computers

I was 11 and I didn't have a PC. Many friends had a 386 or even a 486. I was reading PC magazines -PC World is the one I remember the most- for a year or more already. It started out of pure interest, from a child that looked at those machines like something magic. Maybe the reason was the good memories I had with the Spectrum ZX of my uncle when my cousin and I were 8 years old. But I didn't have a computer, it was what it was. Expensive thing those computers.

And then, two days before my birthday I was at home playing with a friend or doing whatever kids did those days. I am probably to old to really remember. Someone rang the bell, there was confussion (on my side), it was a family's friend with two boxes. A picture of an IBM compatible PC, 90's style in Spain. I don't remember much from that moment, just a few things:
- I told my friend "let's talk tomorrow". Somehow unpolite, but we kept being friends, so he probably understood.
- the man who brought the PC showed me how to install it while installing it
- I got on the MS-DOS command line with the manual I was already reading (and dreaming about the momentI would have a pc to use it)

And that's it. I do not remember when he left, what I did, if there was some game availabe in the disks this family's friend brought or anything else that happened that day. I am sure I was just absorted in playing with the black screen, the incantations that were called commands and some basic software that came with it like Windows 3.1 and Paint that was included.
Since that day, many years ago, I was always interested in computers, tinkering things, understanding how it works by changing stuff, breaking stuff and then trying to fix the stuff I broke. 

I did that to MS-DOS after a couple of months. I broke it by messing around with init files, if I recall correctly. I fixed it by reinstalling the whole OS. That's how I learnt how to format the hard drive, how to install MS-DOS from scratch, how to write .BAT files. And it was a great way to learn. Exciting and challenging, just by playing with the computer and trying to figure out what where its parts. If I think of today's computer usage and how the apps work as well as the OS, it seems it is way more difficult to learn that way. There are too many abstractions on top of everything to make it easier to use, which is good in that sense, but at the same time I feel something was lost. When things broke on DOS or early computers, you could try to fix it, learning by doing, and the result was more interesting as it was YOU the one who solved it.

Those first steps and "adventure" discovering how that piece of technology worked were fundamental in me getting hooked by computers and wanting to know more. So some time later, I learnt some C thanks to another friend of the family who was a programmer, using the software that I have best memories from: **Borland Turbo C++**. In all honesty, I don't think I was able to actually make anything that worked, beyond the classical *Hello World* and maybe some calculator thing. It doesn't matter, I remember enjoying every second of it. The clear impression was: I am not only able to use programs in the computer... I can even tell the computer what I WANT it to do!

It was a matter of time I got stuck with learning programming, at least, practicing the part of coding. But I have been playing with computers since then, even during the time I got tired of them for a while due to my frustration with Windows 95. It was not about the system itself, I just missed my real MS-DOS command line, my writing of .BAT files, decyphering arj commands to compress and uncompress large programs and games... **adventures in front of a keyboard**. That nostalgia is what made me writing this in the new released ***edit*** text editor that Microsoft released.

Many other technologies, software, programming languages and operative systems after that, I am still in love with computers. Trying to learn more, new things, old things... And it just felt right to make this the first post of this personal diary.
